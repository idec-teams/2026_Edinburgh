Computational methods

Dry lab: structural prediction and docking

DL-1

AlphaFold3 structure prediction

Confirmed View results →

Full-length CxnA protein structure was predicted using AlphaFold3 (Google DeepMind, 2024) via the publicly accessible AlphaFold Server. Separate predictions were run for the GH10/Cex catalytic domain and the GH1-fold domain to obtain higher-resolution models of each active site.

Parameters

  • Input: CxnA amino acid sequence (full-length; domain boundaries as per Duedu & French 2016)
  • Model version: AlphaFold3 (server, accessed 2025)
  • Templates: Used where available (default server settings)
  • Number of recycles: Default (3)
  • Output: mmCIF structure files, pLDDT confidence scores, PAE (Predicted Aligned Error) matrix

Output quality assessment

pLDDT scores above 90 in the catalytic core of both GH10 and GH1 domains indicate high per-residue confidence. Regions connecting domains (linker segments) showed lower pLDDT (60–75), consistent with expected flexibility. PAE plots confirmed low inter-domain error for active-site residues.

Note on AlphaFold3 vs AlphaFold2 for this application

AlphaFold3 was selected over AlphaFold2 for its improved modelling of ligand-binding pockets and multi-chain assemblies. For the purposes of structure validation and docking grid preparation, the single-chain prediction is sufficient. AlphaFold3 predictions should be treated as computational models, not experimental structures — hence the RMSD validation against 1FHD (Method DL-2) as a critical quality check before proceeding to docking.

DL-2

Structural validation: RMSD against 1FHD crystal structure

Confirmed View results →

To assess the accuracy of the AlphaFold3 GH10/Cex model, it was structurally superimposed against the published X-ray crystal structure of the Cex exoglucanase from Cellulomonas fimi (PDB: 1FHD, Notenboom et al., 1998).

Alignment procedure

  • Tool: PyMOL v2.5 (Schrödinger) — align command with default parameters
  • Alignment basis: Cα atoms of the GH10 catalytic domain, residues range pending
  • Outlier removal: Standard PyMOL iterative rejection (5 cycles, 2σ cutoff)
  • Key result: RMSD = 0.293 Å over aligned Cα atoms

Interpretation

An RMSD of 0.293 Å is well within the range expected for near-identical structures (typically <1.0 Å for the same protein fold). This result gives high confidence that the AlphaFold3 model accurately represents the GH10/Cex active-site geometry and that downstream docking predictions are structurally grounded. See Results for the full overlay figure and per-residue deviation plot.

DL-3

Molecular docking: Autodock Vina

Confirmed View results →

Cellobiose was docked into both the GH10/Cex and GH1-fold active sites using Autodock Vina 1.2. Xylobiose was also docked into GH10 as a comparative substrate (see caveat below).

Protocol

  • Receptor preparation: AlphaFold3 mmCIF → PDB format. Hydrogens added using AutoDockTools 1.5.7. Water molecules removed. Gasteiger charges assigned.
  • Ligand preparation: Cellobiose SMILES retrieved from PubChem (CID 10712). 3D conformer generated using Open Babel. Torsion bonds set free.
  • Docking grid: Centered on the catalytic glutamates (GLU127/GLU233 for GH10; GLU350/GLU404 for GH1). Grid box: 20 × 20 × 20 Å, spacing 0.375 Å.
  • Exhaustiveness: 32 (increased from default 8 for greater sampling)
  • Number of poses: 20 retained per run
  • Replicate runs: 3 per target to assess pose reproducibility

Critical caveat: absolute docking scores

Autodock Vina binding free energy scores (kcal/mol) are semi-empirical estimates and systematically overestimate true binding affinity in many cases. They are reliable for rank-ordering poses of the same ligand within the same receptor (relative comparison), but absolute scores should not be taken as accurate predictions of experimental ΔG.

Worked example of this limitation: Docking of xylobiose into GH10 yields a Vina score value pending kcal/mol more favourable than cellobiose, despite cellobiose being the natural substrate. This discrepancy illustrates that improved docking scores do not automatically indicate biologically superior substrates, and that experimental validation is always required.

Confirmed View results →

Protein–ligand interactions for the top-ranked docking poses were analysed using PLIP (Protein–Ligand Interaction Profiler, Adasme et al., 2021) and visualised using PoseView (BioSolveIT).

Interactions characterised

  • Hydrogen bonds (donor–acceptor pairs, distances, angles)
  • Hydrophobic contacts (Cβ–ligand distances ≤ 4.0 Å)
  • Stacking interactions (π–π, T-shaped)
  • Salt bridges (charge–charge)

DL-5

Pose convergence analysis

Confirmed View results →

To assess docking reliability, the 20 retained poses per target were clustered by RMSD (cutoff: 2.0 Å) to evaluate convergence. High convergence (poses clustering tightly) indicates that the docking algorithm consistently identifies the same binding geometry. Pose scatter indicates ambiguity.

GH10: Tight clustering of poses (see Results for RMSD distribution histogram). This supports high confidence in the predicted binding geometry for downstream PLIP analysis.

GH1: Poses show greater scatter across the domain surface. This is flagged as requiring further investigation — it may indicate that the GH1-fold does not possess a well-defined cellobiose-binding pocket in the current structural model, consistent with the absence of GH1-type activity in wild-type CxnA. This result is not overclaimed. The scatter is noted as an open question that wet-lab results will need to address.

WL-2

Error-prone PCR (epPCR) mutagenesis library generation

In Progress Results (pending) →

Error-prone PCR introduces random nucleotide substitutions at a controlled rate across the target gene. We use the Mn²⁺-dNTP imbalance method to bias toward a target mutation rate of approximately 1–3 amino acid changes per gene, which empirically maximises the probability of finding beneficial single-site and double-site mutations without excessive fitness loss from multiple deleterious changes.

epPCR protocol

  • Polymerase: Taq (no proofreading), 5 U/reaction
  • Buffer: Standard Taq buffer + 0.5 mM MnCl₂
  • dNTP imbalance: 200 µM dATP, 200 µM dGTP, 1 mM dCTP, 1 mM dTTP
  • Template: 1 ng plasmid carrying wild-type CxnA
  • Primers: Flanking the target region with 20-mer tails matching vector sequence
  • Cycles: 35 cycles (94°C 30s / 55°C 30s / 72°C 90s)
  • Library size target: ≥10³ unique colonies

Library validation

  • 10 random colonies sequenced to determine empirical mutation rate
  • Acceptance criterion: average 1–4 bp substitutions per gene
  • Libraries with >6 bp average substitutions are discarded and repeated

Project-specific targeting (informed by DL-3/DL-4)

For Project A, epPCR is prioritised over the GH1-fold region (residues range pending), informed by the docking and PLIP analysis identifying GLU350, GLU404, GLN87, and TRP281 as key positions. For Project B, epPCR covers the full GH10 domain.

WL-4

Primary selection

In Progress Results (pending) →

Project A — cellobiose sole-carbon selection

Transformants are plated onto M9 minimal medium with cellobiose (10 mM) as the sole carbon source. Wild-type E. coli cannot metabolise cellobiose; colonies that grow have either acquired or up-regulated the ability to cleave cellobiose, selecting for beta-glucosidase activity (or phosphotransferase system import — see screening step WL-5 for discrimination).

Project B — CMC plate clearing

Transformants are plated on LB + 0.5% carboxymethylcellulose (CMC) + IPTG. After 24 h growth, plates are stained with Congo Red (0.1%, 15 min) and destained with 1 M NaCl. Clear halos around colonies indicate CMCase (endoglucanase) activity. Halo diameter is proportional to activity level, enabling direct visual screening for improved endoglucanase variants.

WL-5

Secondary screening: fluorogenic enzyme assays

In Progress Results (pending) →

Colonies passing primary selection are screened in liquid culture with fluorogenic substrates. 4-methylumbelliferyl (MU) substrates release the fluorophore 4-methylumbelliferone (MU, excitation 360 nm, emission 450 nm) upon hydrolysis, enabling quantitative measurement of specific enzymatic activities.

SubstrateAbbreviationActivity detectedRelevance
4-methylumbelliferyl-β-D-glucopyranoside MUG Beta-glucosidase Project A (primary screen)
4-methylumbelliferyl-β-D-cellobioside MUC Exoglucanase / cellobiohydrolase Project B
Carboxymethylcellulose + DNS assay CMC-DNS Endoglucanase Project B (quantitative)

Assay conditions

  • Substrate concentration: 1 mM MUG/MUC in 50 mM sodium phosphate buffer, pH 7.0
  • Enzyme source: Clarified cell lysate (sonication, 4°C, 20 min, 15,000 × g)
  • Reaction volume: 200 µL in 96-well flat-bottom plate
  • Incubation: 37°C, 60 min
  • Stop: 50 µL 1 M Na₂CO₃ (raises pH, maximises MU fluorescence)
  • Measurement: Fluorescence plate reader, Ex 360 nm / Em 450 nm
  • Controls: Wild-type CxnA (positive), empty vector (negative), substrate blank
