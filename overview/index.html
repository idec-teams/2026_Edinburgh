Project Overview

Engineering CxnA toward trifunctionality

CxnA is a natural two-in-one cellulase. We are using directed evolution to try to add a third catalytic activity — and to improve the two it already has.

In plain language

What are we actually doing?

Cellulose — the structural material in plant cell walls and one of the most abundant organic substances on Earth — is made of long chains of glucose molecules. Breaking those chains all the way down to glucose (usable for fermentation and biofuel production) requires three different types of enzyme working together.

Most engineered systems express all three as separate proteins, which is inefficient. Separate enzymes floating in solution don't always find their substrate at the right time, and producing multiple proteins is more expensive than producing one.

CxnA is an existing enzyme from the soil bacterium Cellulomonas fimi that already combines two of these activities in a single protein chain — an exoglucanase and an endoglucanase — making it naturally bifunctional. It was originally characterised by Duedu & French at the University of Edinburgh in 2016. [1]

This project asks: can we go further? Can we evolve CxnA to also perform the third activity — beta-glucosidase, the enzyme that converts the disaccharide cellobiose into two molecules of glucose — creating a fully trifunctional enzyme? And separately, can we use directed evolution to improve the two activities CxnA already has, making it an overall better cellulose-degrading enzyme?

The three activities of cellulose degradation

  1. 1
    Exoglucanase

    Attacks crystalline cellulose from chain ends, releasing the disaccharide cellobiose.

    CxnA WT — confirmed
  2. 2
    Endoglucanase

    Cleaves β-1,4 glycosidic bonds within the cellulose chain, creating new accessible chain ends.

    CxnA WT — confirmed
  3. 3
    Beta-glucosidase

    Converts cellobiose into two glucose molecules. The final step to usable fuel.

    Project A target — evolving
Technical framing: domain architecture and mechanistic rationale

CxnA contains two distinct glycoside hydrolase (GH) domains: a GH10 domain (bearing both Cex-type exoglucanase and CenA-type endoglucanase activities) and a GH1-fold domain that is currently catalytically inactive relative to beta-glucosidase function in the wild-type protein.

GH1 family beta-glucosidases use a retaining double-displacement mechanism, with two conserved glutamate residues acting as the catalytic nucleophile and general acid/base. The structural analysis in this project (see Results — Dry Lab) identifies candidate residues GLU350 and GLU404 as potential catalytic positions in the GH1-fold domain of CxnA, and characterises a steric barrier involving GLN87 and TRP281 that may need to be overcome for cellobiose binding.

There is no direct published precedent for evolving a GH10 scaffold to acquire GH1-type beta-glucosidase activity. [2] This project tests that gap. The honest prior expectation is low; the experimental and computational framing is designed to generate interpretable data regardless of whether the primary evolution succeeds.

Two parallel research tracks

Project A & Project B

Both tracks run simultaneously with shared reagents and infrastructure.

Project A In Progress

Trifunctional evolution: adding a third activity

Goal: Evolve beta-glucosidase (GH1-type) activity into the GH1-fold domain of CxnA.

Strategy

  • Error-prone PCR (epPCR) on the GH1-fold region of CxnA to generate a mutant library
  • Selection by growth on cellobiose as the sole carbon source
  • Screening of surviving colonies using the fluorogenic substrate 4-methylumbelliferyl-β-D-glucopyranoside (MUG)
  • Computational guidance from AlphaFold3 docking to target the active-site region

Honest framing

No direct literature precedent exists for this specific evolutionary transition. The project is designed to generate interpretable data at every stage — including if evolution fails, characterising why is a result.

Dry lab inputs

AlphaFold3 structure of the GH1 domain · Cellobiose docking poses · PLIP interaction analysis identifying GLU350, GLU404 as candidate catalytic residues · Steric barrier characterisation (GLN87/TRP281)

Project B In Progress

Activity enhancement: improving what CxnA already does

Goal: Improve the kinetics and/or thermostability of CxnA's existing exoglucanase and endoglucanase activities.

Strategy

  • epPCR across the full CxnA coding sequence (or targeted to the GH10 domain)
  • CMC (carboxymethylcellulose) plate clearing assay for endoglucanase activity
  • MUC (4-methylumbelliferyl-β-D-cellobioside) assay for exoglucanase activity
  • Direct comparison of mutant activity to wild-type CxnA baseline

Rationale

Project B provides an interpretable positive control: we are evolving activity that demonstrably already exists. This ensures the selection and screening system is validated even if Project A yields no active mutants.

Dry lab inputs

AlphaFold3 structure of GH10/Cex domain · Cellobiose and xylobiose docking · PLIP interaction map of GLU127, GLU233 · Tight pose convergence in GH10 supporting high confidence in the binding geometry

Broader context

Why does this matter?

🌿

Biofuel production

Efficient cellulose degradation is a key bottleneck in second-generation biofuel production from agricultural waste, forestry residues, and dedicated energy crops. A self-contained trifunctional enzyme could reduce the enzyme loading required per unit of glucose produced.

♻

Biomass recycling

Converting plant biomass to fermentable sugars has applications in waste valorisation, paper and textile industry bioprocessing, and soil carbon cycling. More efficient single-enzyme solutions lower the barrier to deployment.

🤖

Testing AI-assisted protein engineering

This project explicitly examines where AlphaFold3 structural predictions and computational docking can responsibly guide directed evolution — and where they cannot substitute for real experimental data. The limitations are documented and discussed, not hidden.
