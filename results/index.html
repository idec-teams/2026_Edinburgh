Dry Lab — Structural analysis and docking

DL-R1

AlphaFold3 structure quality & RMSD validation

Confirmed Methods: DL-1, DL-2

AlphaFold3 models were generated for the GH10/Cex domain and the GH1-fold domain of CxnA. The GH10/Cex model was validated against the published crystal structure 1FHD (Notenboom et al., 1998 [3]) by Cα RMSD alignment in PyMOL.

Key Result
RMSD = 0.293 Å
Cα RMSD over 1,958 aligned atoms vs 1FHD crystal structure (Notenboom et al., 1998). This falls within the 0.3–0.4 Å range Notenboom et al. report for crystal-to-crystal Cα deviations in Cex — confirming AlphaFold3 reproduces the active-site geometry as accurately as the crystal structures agree with each other.

Interpretation

An RMSD of 0.293 Å is strong evidence that AlphaFold3 has correctly predicted the active-site geometry of the GH10/Cex domain. This validates the use of the AlphaFold3 model as the receptor for docking studies (DL-R2). The analogous validation for GH1 is limited by the absence of a comparable published crystal structure for the CxnA GH1-fold domain; confidence in the GH1 predictions is therefore based on pLDDT alone.

Cellobiose docking into GH10/Cex active site (Project B)

Confirmed Methods: DL-3, DL-4, DL-5

Autodock Vina docking of cellobiose into the GH10/Cex active site yielded consistent, well-converged poses. PLIP analysis of the top-ranked pose identified hydrogen-bond contacts with the established catalytic residues GLU127 and GLU233, consistent with the expected binding geometry for a GH10 exoglucanase substrate.

DL-R3

Cellobiose docking into GH1-fold active site (Project A)

Confirmed Methods: DL-3, DL-4, DL-5

Cellobiose was docked into the GH1-fold domain of CxnA (the Project A target). The docking results differ significantly from GH10, with important implications for the directed evolution strategy.

Steric barrier identified
GLN87 & TRP281
These two residues form a steric barrier limiting cellobiose access to the candidate active-site pocket (GLU350/GLU404). This is a key finding for Project A: directed evolution may need to modify or displace these residues to permit productive substrate binding. This is a hypothesis, not a proven requirement.

Honest interpretation — what this does and does not tell us

The GH1 docking results do not demonstrate that the GH1-fold domain of CxnA is incapable of binding cellobiose, nor do they prove it is capable. They identify structural features that computational methods suggest may impede binding. Whether those features are actually barriers to activity — and whether directed evolution can overcome them — is an empirical question that only wet-lab experiments can answer. The steric barrier finding guides but does not constrain the epPCR targeting strategy.

DL-R4

Docking score caveat: xylobiose ΔG comparison

Confirmed Methods: DL-3

Xylobiose (a xylose dimer analogous in structure to cellobiose) was docked into the GH10/Cex active site as a comparative substrate. This experiment serves as an explicit test of docking score interpretability.

Caveat demonstrated
Vina overestimates by ~3.3 kcal/mol
The experimentally measured ΔG for xylobiose in Cex (its preferred natural substrate) is −3.29 kcal/mol (Ki = 4800 µM; Notenboom et al., 2000 [3]). Vina predicts −6.6 kcal/mol for cellobiose in the same enzyme — a ~3.3 kcal/mol overestimate. Absolute Vina scores must not be used as direct substitutes for experimental ΔG measurements.
📊
Figure: Cellobiose vs xylobiose Vina scores in GH10

Bar chart comparing top-pose Vina ΔG for cellobiose vs xylobiose. Illustrates score unreliability for substrate identity.

figures/drylab/cellobiose_vs_xylobiose_scores.png

Methodological note — all Vina scores on this site

Throughout this project, Autodock Vina scores are used only for within-receptor, within-ligand pose ranking and convergence assessment. They are not used to draw conclusions about whether one substrate is preferred over another, nor to predict absolute affinity values. All such conclusions require experimental validation.

DL-R5

Pose convergence analysis: GH10 vs GH1

Confirmed Methods: DL-5

Pose convergence was assessed by clustering all 20 retained docking poses per target by pairwise RMSD (cutoff 2.0 Å). The GH10 and GH1 results show strikingly different patterns.

GH10 / Cex
Tight convergence

The majority of the 20 poses cluster within 2 Å RMSD of the top-ranked pose. This indicates that Autodock Vina consistently identifies the same binding geometry, supporting high confidence in the predicted GH10–cellobiose binding mode and the PLIP analysis based on it.

📊
Figure: GH10 pose RMSD histogram

figures/drylab/gh10_pose_convergence.png

GH1-fold
Scattered — investigation ongoing

GH1 poses are distributed across multiple clusters with no dominant pose. This indicates that Autodock Vina does not identify a clear, preferred binding site for cellobiose in the GH1-fold domain, consistent with the absence of a pre-formed binding pocket in the wild-type structure.

This result is not over-interpreted. Pose scatter does not prove that GH1 cannot bind cellobiose — it indicates computational ambiguity. It is flagged as an open question and a motivation for the directed evolution approach: if structural changes in the binding pocket are needed, directed evolution may identify them.
